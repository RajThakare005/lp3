PRACTICAL 17 - SURVEY REPORT ON TYPES OF BLOCKCHAINS AND REAL-WORLD USE CASES
=============================================================================

AIM
Study different types of blockchains and identify their real-world use cases.

INTRODUCTION
A blockchain is a distributed ledger in which records are grouped into blocks and
linked using cryptographic hashes. Copies of the ledger are maintained by multiple
participants. Consensus mechanisms help participants agree on the ledger state.

TYPES OF BLOCKCHAINS

1. PUBLIC BLOCKCHAIN
- Open network that people can generally read and participate in according to its rules.
- Usually permissionless for joining and transaction submission.
- Examples: Bitcoin and Ethereum.
- Uses: cryptocurrency transfers, public smart contracts, token issuance and
  decentralized applications.
- Challenges: public data visibility, scalability and variable transaction fees.

2. PRIVATE BLOCKCHAIN
- Controlled by one organization or administrator.
- Participation and write access are restricted.
- Uses: internal business records, controlled inter-department workflows and
  enterprise audit trails.
- Benefit: access control and governance can be tailored to the organization.
- Limitation: it is less decentralized than a public blockchain.

3. CONSORTIUM / FEDERATED BLOCKCHAIN
- Operated by a group of selected organizations rather than one organization alone.
- Permissioned membership and shared governance.
- Uses: inter-bank settlement, trade finance, supply-chain coordination and
  shared records among business partners.
- Benefit: multiple organizations can share a governed ledger.
- Challenge: members must agree on governance, permissions and operating rules.

4. HYBRID BLOCKCHAIN
- Combines private/permissioned components with selected public blockchain features.
- Sensitive records can remain under controlled access while selected proofs or
  transactions are made publicly verifiable.
- Uses: regulated data workflows, identity systems and supply-chain proofs.
- Challenge: integration and privacy design can be complex.

REAL-WORLD USE CASES
1. FINANCE AND PAYMENTS
   Shared settlement records, digital assets and programmable payment workflows.
2. SUPPLY-CHAIN MANAGEMENT
   Recording product origin, custody changes and shipment milestones across partners.
3. HEALTHCARE
   Audit trails for access to records and proofs of data integrity. Sensitive medical
   data should not simply be placed on a public chain.
4. DIGITAL IDENTITY
   Verifiable credentials and proofs that help users present identity attributes.
5. LAND AND ASSET REGISTRIES
   Tamper-evident records and tokenized representations, subject to legal recognition.
6. DIGITAL CERTIFICATES
   Issuing and verifying academic or training certificates.
7. ENERGY
   Recording energy certificates, renewable-energy credits or peer-to-peer
   energy-trading transactions in suitable systems.
8. DIGITAL OWNERSHIP
   Tokens can represent digital items or claims, but legal ownership depends on
   the underlying rights and applicable law.

ADVANTAGES
- Shared and tamper-evident record keeping.
- Traceability and auditability.
- Programmable business rules through smart contracts.
- Reduced need for one party to maintain the only authoritative database in
  multi-party workflows.

LIMITATIONS
- Scalability and transaction costs can be concerns.
- Public-chain transactions may be difficult to reverse.
- Privacy and data-protection requirements need careful architecture.
- Smart-contract bugs can cause loss or incorrect behaviour.
- Blockchain is not necessary when one trusted organization can manage a normal
  database more efficiently.

CONCLUSION
Public, private, consortium and hybrid blockchains offer different permission and
governance models. Their suitability depends on trust, privacy, performance,
regulatory needs and the number of participating organizations.
